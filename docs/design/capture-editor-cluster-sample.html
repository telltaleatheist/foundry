The two that read alike before now differ by the word that matters: one says turn and names the other 24; the other says crop and names all 25. Neither needs anybody to explain it.

One thing only you can decide

The checkbox is gone. What has to replace it is what “apply to all” does to a page you already fixed by hand, and there are two honest answers:

A — leave your work alone. Applying to all skips the pages you set yourself and says so afterwards (“left alone: IMG_0212”). Nothing you did by hand is ever overwritten by a press. To redo one, you open it and fix it.

B — the newest press wins. Applying to all changes every page, including ones you set. Simpler to explain, and it means a global you get right the second time really does reach everything.

Today’s behaviour is A with the checkbox as an escape hatch. Say which one “assumed” means and it gets built with no checkbox either way.

What this mock does not change

The picture and its corner handles are exactly as built — dragging corners, the gutter and its two ends all still work the way they do now. The two stages are untouched: the first pass offers one act, and after that you get the per-page one as well. Mint is still on the light table’s rail and not in here.

When this one looks right

Every photograph was shot the same way, so one gutter places them all.

“I clicked split spreads and it looked no different from place crop.”

It didn’t look different because there was nothing to look at. The line and its two knobs are drawn only once a photograph already has a split, and a photograph you have never split has none — so choosing the tool changed which buttons were on screen and left the picture exactly as it was. The one control that would have made a line was a small button among four others.

The line comes first now. Choosing Split puts it down the middle of the sheet, upright, with a knob at each end, before you spend a single gesture. That is the tool looking like what it is. You drag the ends onto the gutter and then cut — the picture shows the proposal, the button performs it.

A second thing worth your opinion

This mock treats the line as a proposal: it appears, you place it, and the photograph becomes two pages when you press Cut.

The alternative is that choosing Split cuts immediately down the middle and dragging the line just moves the gutter afterwards. That is one press fewer and it means the tool does what its name says the instant you pick it — but it changes the page count of the book the moment you click a tool to have a look, and there is no un-cut today.

The mock takes the cautious one. Say the word and it becomes the other.

What is not being guessed at: whether the rail row actually hands this tool to the editor at all is being measured separately, not designed around. If it turns out the row never arrived, this screen is still what it should look like when it does.
