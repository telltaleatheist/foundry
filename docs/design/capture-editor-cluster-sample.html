The two that read alike before now differ by the word that matters: one says turn and names the other 24; the other says crop and names all 25. Neither needs anybody to explain it.

And the third one, which you could not find because it was often not there

“Turn all by the same amount” does not exist on a fresh shoot at all, and once it does appear it stays greyed out until you have turned the photograph in front of you. Worse, it goes dead again the moment you step to the next page and back — it counts the turns you made in this visit, so walking away forgets them even though the page stays turned. There is no way to learn any of that from the surface, which is most of why it read as a button that did nothing.

Proposed: it is always in the Turn tool, and it asks a question about the book rather than about your visit — “Turn the other 24 to match this one”. It compares this photograph’s orientation with theirs, so it means the same thing whether you turned it ten seconds ago or last Tuesday, and it is only unavailable when the other 24 already match (with the button saying so rather than going grey in silence).

Settled — the checkbox, and what replaced it

Your words: “apply to the ones i did by hand, give me a modal that asks if i want to override hand-picked settings or something.”

So Apply to all means all, and the question moves to the one moment it is a real question: when there is actually something of yours to overwrite. No hand-placed pages, no modal — see the Overriding your own work tab.

What this mock does not change

The picture and its corner handles are exactly as built — dragging corners, the gutter and its two ends all still work the way they do now. The two stages are untouched: the first pass offers one act, and after that you get the per-page one as well. Mint is still on the light table’s rail and not in here.

When this one looks right

Every photograph was shot the same way, so one gutter places them all.

“I clicked split spreads and it looked no different from place crop.”

It didn’t look different because there was nothing to look at. The line and its two knobs are drawn only once a photograph already has a split, and a photograph you have never split has none — so choosing the tool changed which buttons were on screen and left the picture exactly as it was. The one control that would have made a line was a small button among four others.

The line comes first now. Choosing Split puts it down the middle of the sheet, upright, with a knob at each end, before you spend a single gesture. That is the tool looking like what it is. You drag the ends onto the gutter and then cut — the picture shows the proposal, the button performs it.

A second thing worth your opinion

This mock treats the line as a proposal: it appears, you place it, and the photograph becomes two pages when you press Cut.

The alternative is that choosing Split cuts immediately down the middle and dragging the line just moves the gutter afterwards. That is one press fewer and it means the tool does what its name says the instant you pick it — but it changes the page count of the book the moment you click a tool to have a look, and there is no un-cut today.

The mock takes the cautious one. Say the word and it becomes the other.

What is not being guessed at: whether the rail row actually hands this tool to the editor at all is being measured separately, not designed around. If it turns out the row never arrived, this screen is still what it should look like when it does.

This only ever appears when there is something of yours to lose

No pages set by hand, no question — you press Use this crop on all 25 and it happens. The checkbox asked every single time about a situation that usually does not exist; this asks once, at the only moment it is a real question, and names which three.

The second sentence is not decoration. Applying a crop copies the orientation too, so answering yes turns those three the way this one sits. That is the thing a person would not guess, and it is the thing they would notice an hour later.

One small thing to confirm

Two buttons, two outcomes — but what should the ✕ or Escape do? The mock treats it as “do nothing at all”, on the usual rule that closing a question cancels it. The alternative is that closing means Leave those 3 alone, since that is the gentler of the two answers. Say which and it is one line.

“I rotated one but it didn’t stick apparently?”

It stuck. All 25 of your pages are turned a quarter, right now, in the file — the measurement went and counted them. The turn reached the recipe, the file, and the printed page box of the PDF.

What it never reached was your eyes. The table drew the photograph exactly as the camera took it and marked the turn with a dot four hundredths of a card wide. So a book you had turned upright still looked sideways on the table you were standing on.

Today

Every card the shape the camera gave it. The only difference between turned and untouched is the small dot moving from one corner to another.

Proposed

The card draws the page the way round it will be printed. A turned spread stands up. You can see the whole book is upright from across the room, which is the question you were actually asking.

The word turned on the card is the smaller half of it; the shape is what carries. Both are in the mock so you can say if the word is worth keeping or is clutter once the card itself is honest.
